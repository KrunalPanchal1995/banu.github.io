What you will be able to do

  • tell a discriminative model p(y|x) from a generative model p(x), and say what each can and cannot do;
  • write the training objective of an autoregressive model, an autoencoder, a VAE, a GAN and a diffusion model in one line each;
  • derive the VAE evidence lower bound and the GAN optimal discriminator, and explain the reparameterisation trick;
  • build a character-level text generator and read its perplexity on seen and unseen text;
  • explain hallucination, bias, privacy and copyright risk as consequences of the training objective, with a measured example.

The big picture

Most machine learning you have met is discriminative: draw a boundary between classes. Generative AI asks for more: learn what the data looks like, so you can produce new examples that were never in the training set.

Six ways to model a distribution.
familyidea
autoregressivewrite one piece at a time, each conditioned on the earlier ones
autoencodersqueeze to a short code and rebuild (a compressor, not yet a generator)
VAEautoencoder with a fuzzy code and a prior, so you can sample new codes
GANa forger and a detective improve each other
diffusionlearn to remove noise that was added step by step
Transformerthe neural network inside most of the above (an architecture, not a family)
One objective explains the risks. Generative models are trained to produce plausible data, not true data. That is why they hallucinate, why they mirror biases in their training data, and why a model with enough capacity can copy its training text. In the character model, 60% of 12-letter windows are copied verbatim at memory n = 6.

Run the code yourself

Everything is plain NumPy, including the neural networks (with hand-written backpropagation). After downloading and unzipping the All code (.zip):

pip install -r requirements.txt
python codes/genmodels/genmodels_np/genmodels_np.py               # tiny n-gram demo
python codes/genmodels/genmodels_np/tests/test_genmodels_np.py    # 7 tests incl. gradient checks, prints: ok
python figures/fig04_vae.py                                       # trains the VAE (a few seconds) and reproduces Figure 4

The module has CharNgram, MLP (manual backprop), train_autoencoder, train_vae, train_gan_1d and the classifiers.

What the experiments really showed. The textbook line “generative classifiers need less data” failed at 3 points per class here, because the Gaussian model estimates full covariance matrices. And the best GAN step was 300, not the last one. Both are discussed in the PDF.

Self-check

Answer in your head first, then click to reveal.

Which of p(y|x) and p(x) lets you create new data?
p(x), or p(x|y). p(y|x) alone only labels.
What are the two terms of the VAE loss?
A reconstruction error and a KL term that keeps the codes close to the prior N(0, I).
What does the optimal GAN discriminator output when the generator is perfect?
1/2 everywhere: it cannot tell real from generated.
Why does adding more context to an n-gram model first help and then hurt on new text?
More context fits the training text better but makes the model memorise it; held-out perplexity rises again after n = 4 here.
What does the reparameterisation trick do?
Writes z = mu + sigma * eps with eps independent of the parameters, so gradients can flow through the sampling step.
Name two ways a generative model can leak or misuse training data.
Memorisation (reproducing training text) and hallucination or bias inherited from the data; all follow from what the objective rewards.
